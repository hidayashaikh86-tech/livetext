How to Share Code Securely Between Devices Without an Account

Copying a code snippet from your laptop to your phone used to mean emailing yourself or using a third-party clipboard service that stores your data. Shareli changes that. Here is how developers and students can instantly transfer code across devices using end-to-end encryption with zero data retention.

The Problem with Traditional Methods

When you need to share a code snippet or API key from one device to another, most people resort to emailing themselves, using Google Keep, or pasting into WhatsApp. Each of these methods stores your content on third-party servers, potentially forever. For sensitive information like API keys, database credentials, or private notes, this is a real security risk.

The Shareli Solution

Shareli offers a better way. Here is how to transfer code from your laptop to your phone in under 30 seconds:

  • Open shareli.online on your laptop.
  • Click "Create Private Room" to generate a unique encrypted space.
  • Paste your code snippet into the message box and click Send.
  • Click the QR Code button in the sidebar.
  • Scan the QR code with your phone's camera — your phone instantly joins the room and sees the code.

The entire process takes under 30 seconds. No account, no email, no storage. The code self-destructs based on the timer you set.

Why This is More Secure

Unlike email or Google Keep, Shareli encrypts your content using AES-GCM 256-bit encryption in your browser before it ever reaches the server. The encryption key is embedded in the URL — not stored on the server. This means even the Shareli server cannot read your code. When the timer expires, the message is deleted from server memory with no database record ever created.

5 Ways to Use Shareli You Might Not Know About

Most people use Shareli as a clipboard. But there are at least 5 other powerful ways to use it — from classroom file drops to temporary team noticeboards.

1. Classroom File Drop

Teachers and students can create a private Shareli room, share the QR code on a projector, and instantly distribute notes, code exercises, or links to the entire class. No learning management system needed.

2. Cross-Device Clipboard

Copy something on your phone and need it on your laptop immediately? Open Shareli on both devices in the public room, paste the text and send. It appears on your other device instantly. Much faster than AirDrop or Bluetooth.

3. One-Time Secure Note

Need to send a password or private key to a colleague? Send it through a Shareli private room with a 1-minute timer. Once they read it, it is gone. No email trail, no chat history.

4. Temporary Team Noticeboard

Create a private Shareli room for your team meeting. Pin important links or notes. Set a 1-hour timer. When the meeting is over, all the information disappears automatically, keeping your communication clean and secure.

5. Anonymous Feedback Box

Share a private room link with your team or class and ask them to drop anonymous feedback. Since Shareli does not track identities, responses are genuinely anonymous — no accounts, no IP logs.

What is End-to-End Encryption and Why Does Shareli Use It?

End-to-end encryption (E2EE) is one of the strongest privacy technologies available. Here is a plain-language explanation of what it means, how it works, and why Shareli chose it over simpler approaches.

What is End-to-End Encryption?

End-to-end encryption means that your data is encrypted on your device and can only be decrypted by the intended recipient. Nobody in between — not internet service providers, not the platform's server, not even the platform's developers — can read the content.

Think of it like a locked box. You lock the content with a key, send the locked box, and only the recipient who has the matching key can open it. Everyone else just sees a locked box.

How Shareli Implements E2EE

Shareli uses the WebCrypto API — a powerful encryption engine built into every modern browser. When you create a private room, the browser generates a unique AES-GCM 256-bit encryption key. This key is stored in your room URL (the part after the # symbol). When you send a message, it is encrypted in your browser using this key before it is sent to the server. The server stores only the encrypted blob — it has no access to the key and therefore no ability to read your message.

Why This Matters for You

Traditional messaging platforms store your messages in a database that they can technically read. Shareli cannot do this by design. Even in a worst-case scenario, the Shareli server could only provide encrypted data that is impossible to read without the room URL that only you shared with your recipient.
